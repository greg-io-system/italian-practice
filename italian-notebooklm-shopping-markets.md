# Shopping & Markets in Italy: Prices, Quantities, and Conversation

## How Shopping Works in Italy

Italian shops and markets have their own rhythms. Many small shops still close for a long lunch break (roughly 1pm–3:30pm) and reopen until 7:30pm or later. Markets (mercati) are typically morning affairs, wrapping up by 1pm or 2pm. Supermarkets follow normal hours. At market stalls and small shops, you often can't touch the produce — you tell the vendor what you want and they select it for you. This is a key cultural difference.

## Situation 1: At the Open-Air Market (Il Mercato)

### Approaching a stall
- Vendor: "Buongiorno! Dica!" — Good morning! What would you like?
- "Buongiorno! Vorrei dei pomodori." — Good morning! I'd like some tomatoes.
- "Mi dà un chilo di pesche, per favore." — Give me a kilo of peaches, please.
- "Mezzo chilo di fragole." — Half a kilo of strawberries.
- "Un etto di prosciutto crudo." — 100 grams of prosciutto. (Un etto = 100g, very common unit)
- "Due etti di parmigiano." — 200 grams of parmesan.

### Asking about items
- "Cosa sono questi?" — What are these?
- "Sono freschi?" — Are they fresh?
- "Di dove sono?" — Where are they from? (origin of produce)
- "Sono locali." — They're local.
- "Li posso assaggiare?" — Can I taste them?

### Numbers and prices at the market
- "Quanto costano i pomodori?" — How much are the tomatoes?
- "Due euro al chilo." — Two euros per kilo.
- "Quanto costa questo?" — How much is this?
- "Tre euro e cinquanta." — Three fifty.
- "Basta così, grazie." — That's enough, thanks. (when they're weighing something)
- "Ne vorrei un po' di più." — I'd like a bit more.
- "Ne vorrei un po' di meno." — I'd like a bit less.

### Finishing up
- "Quant'è in tutto?" — How much is it all together?
- "Sono otto euro e settanta." — That's eight euros seventy.
- "Ho solo una banconota da venti." — I only have a twenty-euro bill.
- "Ha spiccioli?" — Do you have small change?
- "Grazie mille, arrivederci!" — Thank you so much, goodbye!

## Situation 2: In a Shop (Un Negozio)

### Entering and browsing
- Shop assistant: "Buongiorno, posso aiutarla?" — Good morning, can I help you?
- "Sto solo guardando, grazie." — I'm just looking, thanks.
- "Cerco un regalo per mia moglie/mio marito." — I'm looking for a gift for my wife/husband.
- "Avete delle borse in pelle?" — Do you have leather bags?
- "Posso vedere quello/quella?" — Can I see that one? (masculine/feminine)

### Asking about sizes and colors
- "Ce l'avete in un'altra taglia?" — Do you have it in another size?
- "Ce l'avete in un altro colore?" — Do you have it in another color?
- "Posso provarlo/provarla?" — Can I try it on? (masculine/feminine item)
- "Dov'è il camerino?" — Where's the fitting room?
- "È troppo grande/piccolo." — It's too big/small.
- "Ce l'avete più grande/più piccolo?" — Do you have it bigger/smaller?

### Colors you'll need
- nero/nera = black
- bianco/bianca = white
- rosso/rossa = red
- blu = blue
- verde = green
- marrone = brown
- giallo/gialla = yellow
- grigio/grigia = gray

### Deciding and paying
- "Lo/La prendo." — I'll take it. (masculine/feminine)
- "Quanto costa?" — How much does it cost?
- "Costa quarantacinque euro." — It costs forty-five euros.
- "È un po' caro. Fa uno sconto?" — It's a bit expensive. Can you give a discount? (appropriate at markets, not shops)
- "Posso pagare con la carta?" — Can I pay by card?
- "Posso avere una busta?" — Can I have a bag?
- "Mi fa un pacchetto regalo?" — Can you gift-wrap it?

## Situation 3: At the Gelateria (Ice Cream Shop)

This is its own art form in Italy.

- "Che gusti avete?" — What flavors do you have?
- "Posso assaggiare il pistacchio?" — Can I taste the pistachio?
- "Un cono piccolo/medio/grande." — A small/medium/large cone.
- "Una coppetta con due gusti." — A cup with two flavors.
- "Vorrei cioccolato e stracciatella." — I'd like chocolate and stracciatella.
- "Con panna?" — With cream? (they'll ask — say "Sì!" or "No, grazie.")

### Size-related numbers
- piccolo = small (usually 2 flavors)
- medio = medium (usually 2-3 flavors)
- grande = large (usually 3 flavors)

Prices range from €2.50 for a piccolo to €4-5 for a grande, varying by city.

## Situation 4: At the Tabaccaio (Tobacco Shop / Newsstand)

This is where you buy bus tickets, phone top-ups, stamps, and small essentials.

- "Un biglietto dell'autobus, per favore." — A bus ticket, please.
- "Due biglietti della metro." — Two metro tickets.
- "Un francobollo per gli Stati Uniti." — A stamp for the United States.
- "Una ricarica telefonica da dieci euro." — A ten-euro phone top-up.
- "Ha una cartina della città?" — Do you have a city map?

## Essential Market Math

Get comfortable with these patterns — you'll use them constantly:

- "Un chilo" = 1 kilogram
- "Mezzo chilo" = 500 grams
- "Un etto" = 100 grams (very Italian — used for deli items)
- "Due etti" = 200 grams
- "Un litro" = 1 liter
- "Mezzo litro" = half a liter

Price patterns:
- "Due euro al chilo" = €2 per kilo
- "Cinque euro al pezzo" = €5 each
- "Dieci euro per tre" = €10 for three
- "Venti centesimi l'uno" = 20 cents each

## Cultural Notes for Shopping

1. Always greet when entering ANY shop: "Buongiorno!" It's considered very rude not to.
2. Don't touch fruit and vegetables at market stalls unless invited. Point and ask.
3. Bargaining is acceptable at outdoor markets and antique fairs, but NOT in regular shops.
4. Italian sizes differ from US/UK sizes. Ask to try things on rather than guessing.
5. Many small shops don't accept cards under €5-10. Carry small bills and coins.
6. "Scontrino" is your receipt — technically required by law, and occasionally checked by tax police outside shops.
